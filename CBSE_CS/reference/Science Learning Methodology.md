# **Alex's Science Learning Methodology**

# **Introduction**

This framework outlines a systematic approach to mastering science subjects, especially for core curriculum and competitive examinations. It breaks down learning into three core phases: Task Organization, AI-Augmented Synthesis, and Rigorous Problem Solving. The goal is to provide a general blueprint that can be customized to your specific needs.

# **Phase 1: Task and Syllabus Organization**

**The General Method:**  
Before diving into the material, you need a high-level view of your syllabus, assignments, and exam schedules. Use a digital task manager to organize tasks using semantic lists, color-coded tags, and calendar views to ensure no topic is left behind.

**My Specific Example:**  
I use **TickTick** to manage my study sessions. I organize my core curriculum (PCM & CS) and competitive exam prep (like JEE Advanced and Olympiads) into distinct folders. I use color-coded tags to differentiate between standard coursework and self-study, and rely on the calendar view to time-block my problem-solving sessions.

How to customize: You can use Notion, Todoist, or Google Tasks depending on your preference for layout.

# **Phase 2: AI-Augmented Synthesis and Note-taking**

**The General Method:**  
Reading textbooks passively is inefficient. Aggregate your primary sources (textbooks, foundational notes, and past papers) into a centralized, AI-powered knowledge base. Use custom prompts to generate summaries, cross-reference concepts, and extract key insights tailored to your exam format.

**My Specific Example:**  
I use **NotebookLM** as my primary study platform. I index my standard introductory textbooks, lecture notes, and past papers into it. I then use custom system prompts specifically designed to bridge the gap between fundamental concepts and competitive exam requirements. This allows me to query my own study material interactively.

*How to customize:* You can use tools like ChatGPT, Claude, or standard digital notes (like Obsidian/OneNote) to actively summarize and connect your foundational notes with reference books.

# **Phase 3: Rigorous and Structured Problem Solving**

**The General Method:**  
Science and mathematics require solving progressively harder problems. Move beyond standard introductory texts to advanced problem sets. Most importantly, maintain strict, standardized notations during your problem-solving to avoid cognitive overload and silly mistakes.

**My Specific Example:**  
Once I finish the introductory basics, I move to specialized textbooks like *Pathfinder* or *S.L. Loney*. To keep my derivations and calculations clean, I use a strict personal notation system: for instance, I strictly use the symbol Δ to represent the *target numeric value* to be calculated, while using ∇ or 'curl' for operational vectors. This precise discipline prevents confusion during complex problem-solving.  
---

How to customize: Pick reference books suited to your current level. You don't need my exact notation system, but you should establish your own consistent rules for variables, formulas, and solving steps to minimize errors.

*Note for my friend: This system is just a baseline. Adjust the tools and complexity based on what you're currently preparing for\!*
