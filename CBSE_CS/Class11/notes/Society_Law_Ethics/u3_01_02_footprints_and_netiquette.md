# Unit 3: Society, Law and Ethics (Atoms 01–02)

## 01. Digital Footprints (Active vs. Passive)

- **Mental Model:**
  Imagine walking across fresh wet sand on a beach. Wherever you deliberately stomp, you leave a clear footprint (active). But even if you walk lightly, your shoes leave impressions, drop grains of sand, and trigger motion sensors along the coastal boardwalk without you noticing (passive). On the internet, every click, keystroke, search, and login leaves a permanent trail—some left deliberately, some logged automatically by web servers.

- **Key Facts & CBSE Exam Definitions:**
  - **Digital Footprint:** The unique trail, trace, or record of data left behind by a user while surfing the internet or using digital communication platforms.
  - **Active Digital Footprint:**
    - *Definition:* The data trail created intentionally and deliberately by a user when they consciously share information about themselves online.
    - *Examples:* Posting photos/comments on Instagram or X (Twitter), sending emails, writing a blog post, filling out online registration/admission forms, uploading videos to YouTube.
  - **Passive Digital Footprint:**
    - *Definition:* The data trail generated unintentionally and without the explicit active intervention or awareness of the user while browsing online.
    - *Examples:* Websites recording IP addresses, geolocation data logged by mobile apps, browser history recorded by analytics scripts, tracking cookies stored on hard drives, search engine queries logged by ad servers.
  - **Data Exhaust:** The technical term used for the passive exhaust of data created as a byproduct of digital activities.
  - **Permanence & Impact:** Digital footprints are near-permanent. They form a user's **digital reputation** which can be audited by college admission committees, background-check agencies, and employers.

- **Confusable Pairs: Active vs. Passive Digital Footprint**

| Feature | Active Digital Footprint | Passive Digital Footprint |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **User Awareness / Intent** | **Intentional:** User consciously knows data is being created/shared. | **Unintentional:** User is often unaware that data is being recorded. |
| **Action Trigger** | Form submission, posting, publishing, uploading, sending messages. | Browsing a web page, opening an app, connecting to an IP network. |
| **Storage Mechanism** | Public/private posts, account profiles, messaging databases. | Web server access logs, tracking cookies, HTTP referer headers, telemetry. |
| **Control** | High initial user control (user decides *what* and *when* to post). | Low user control (requires active privacy tools, VPNs, cookie blockers). |
| **CBSE Standard Example** | Publishing a tweet or uploading an Aadhaar scan to a scholarship portal. | A web server logging your device's MAC address, ISP, and geographic city. |

- **Real Indian Examples:**
  1. *E-commerce Retargeting (Passive):* A user searches for "NCERT Class 11 textbooks" on an Indian shopping site like Flipkart without logging in. Within minutes, Google ad banners across unrelated news sites start showing textbook advertisements based on their IP and cookie history.
  2. *Job / Visa Verification (Active):* Consular offices and Indian corporate recruiters reviewing an applicant's public posts on LinkedIn, Facebook, and Instagram to assess candidate conduct and background veracity.
  3. *Aadhaar / DigiLocker Verification (Active):* A student submitting their DigiLocker consent and entering an OTP to pull academic marksheets into the CBSE portal.

- **Common Exam Traps:**
  - *Trap 1:* Confusing "private" browsing with "leaving zero footprint". In Incognito/Private mode, local browser history is not saved on the local device, but the ISP, Wi-Fi router, and destination web servers still log your IP address and activity (passive footprint remains).
  - *Trap 2:* Classifying filling out an online college form as passive because "it's mandatory". It is **active** because the user consciously types and transmits the information.

## 02. Digital Society & Netizen Etiquette: Net, Communication, and Social Media

- **Mental Model:**
  Just as civil society expects you not to shout in a library, shove people in a queue, or spread malicious rumors, the online world expects civil conduct. A **Netizen** (citizen of the net) operates under three distinct sub-codes of conduct: how to use network resources politely (**Net Etiquette**), how to write one-to-one messages professionally (**Communication Etiquette**), and how to behave publicly on shared platforms (**Social Media Etiquette**).

- **Key Facts & CBSE Exam Definitions:**
  - **Netizen:** A citizen of the internet; any person who actively uses the internet for education, communication, commerce, or social interaction.
  - **Digital Etiquette (Netiquette):** A set of rules and informal conventions governing polite, respectful, and ethical behavior online.
  - **Classification of Etiquettes (CBSE Triad):**
    1. **Net Etiquette (General Network & Computing Hygiene):**
       - Respect others' time and bandwidth: do not send massive uncompressed attachments.
       - Do not consume shared institutional bandwidth with personal video streaming or torrent downloads.
       - Respect copyright and intellectual property: do not download or circulate pirated media.
       - Acknowledge source material: provide proper citations when quoting others.
    2. **Communication Etiquette (Direct Messaging, Email, Discussion Forums):**
       - **Do not write in ALL CAPS:** Typing entirely in capital letters is perceived in digital culture as **SHOUTING** or screaming aggressively.
       - Be concise, clear, and professional; always include a meaningful Subject line in emails.
       - Do not spam or forward unverified viral broadcast messages or chain emails.
       - Use appropriate salutations and sign-offs.
       - Respect privacy: do not forward private emails or personal conversations to third parties without consent.
    3. **Social Media Etiquette (Public Broadcasts & Community Platforms):**
       - Think before you post: once posted, content can be screenshot and archived permanently.
       - Do not bully, intimidate, harass, or insult other community members.
       - Do not engage in **trolling** (posting deliberately inflammatory, provocative, or off-topic messages to disrupt discussions).
       - Verify information before sharing to prevent the propagation of fake news and misinformation.
       - Respect intellectual property and personal rights: do not tag or post photos of others without their permission.

- **Confusable Pairs: Communication Etiquette vs. Social Media Etiquette**

| Dimension | Communication Etiquette | Social Media Etiquette |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Audience** | Typically 1-to-1 or bounded groups (email, WhatsApp, Slack, forums). | Mass, public or semi-public broadcasting (Instagram, X, YouTube, Facebook). |
| **Core Risk** | Misinterpreted tone, perceived aggression (ALL CAPS), privacy breach of private messages. | Viral misinformation, public defamation, cyber bullying, permanent reputation damage. |
| **Typical Violations** | Forwarding confidential email chains; replying without subject; typing in all caps. | Creating troll threads; posting unverified rumors; tagging people in embarrassing photos without consent. |

- **Real Indian Examples:**
  1. *WhatsApp University / Viral Hoaxes:* Circulating forwarded messages about fake bank holidays or communal rumors without verification violates basic netizen social media ethics and can violate public safety laws.
  2. *Professional Academic Emailing:* An Indian high school student sending an inquiry to CBSE or an admission dean using an informal email handle like `cool_dude99@gmail.com` with no subject line and body text in ALL CAPS violates communication etiquette.
  3. *Classroom Zoom / Google Meet Behavior:* Students posting abusive memes or playing loud music during live Kendriya Vidyalaya / school online classes violates netiquette and institution code of conduct.

- **Common Exam Traps:**
  - *Trap 1:* Believing that typing in ALL CAPS is used to "show emphasis politely". In standard digital etiquette, writing in ALL CAPS is universally interpreted as hostile shouting.
  - *Trap 2:* Thinking that deleting a social media post erases the ethical/legal liability. Archives (Wayback Machine), screenshots, and server access logs maintain the footprint.
