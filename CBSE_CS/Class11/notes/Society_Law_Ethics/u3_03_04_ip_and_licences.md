# Unit 3: Society, Law and Ethics (Atoms 03–04)

## 03. Intellectual Property Rights (IPR): Copyright, Patent, Trademark, and Violations

- **Mental Model:**
  Think of property. If you buy a plot of land, build a physical house, or buy a car, it is your *tangible property*. Nobody can legally trespass, steal, or copy it. Similarly, when your mind invents a new machine, writes an original book, codes an algorithm, or establishes a distinct brand symbol, that creation of the human intellect is your **Intellectual Property (IP)**. Society grants you legal monopolies (IPR) to prevent others from stealing your mental labor.

- **Key Facts & CBSE Exam Definitions:**
  - **Intellectual Property (IP):** Creations of the human mind, such as inventions, literary and artistic works, designs, symbols, names, software programs, and images used in commerce.
  - **Intellectual Property Rights (IPR):** Legal rights conferred upon the creator or inventor to exclusive exploitation, protection, and commercial benefits of their intellectual creation for a specified duration.
  - **The Three Pillars of IPR:**
    1. **Copyright:**
       - *Protects:* Original creative, artistic, literary, musical, and dramatic works, as well as **computer software code** (source code and object code).
       - *Nature:* Protects the **expression of an idea**, not the abstract idea itself.
       - *Validity in India:* Lifetime of the author plus **60 years** after the author's death (under the Indian Copyright Act, 1957).
       - *Symbol:* ©
    2. **Patent:**
       - *Protects:* Inventions—novel, non-obvious, and industrially useful machines, technological processes, chemical formulations, or hardware apparatus.
       - *Nature:* Grants exclusive right to make, use, sell, or import the patented product/process.
       - *Validity in India:* **20 years** from the filing date of the application (under the Indian Patents Act, 1970).
       - *Note:* In India, mathematical formulas, algorithms, and computer programs *per se* cannot be patented directly (they are protected under copyright), though software embedded with hardware displaying a technical effect may qualify.
    3. **Trademark:**
       - *Protects:* Any visual sign, symbol, word, logo, name, phrase, or design that uniquely identifies and distinguishes the goods or services of one enterprise from those of competitors.
       - *Nature:* Protects brand identity and consumer trust in the marketplace.
       - *Validity in India:* **10 years**, but can be renewed indefinitely every 10 years upon payment of renewal fees (under the Trade Marks Act, 1999).
       - *Symbols:* ™ (unregistered trademark), ® (registered trademark).

- **Confusable Pairs: Copyright vs. Patent vs. Trademark**

| Feature | Copyright | Patent | Trademark |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **What it Protects** | Literary, musical, artistic works, software code. | Technical inventions, novel devices, processes. | Logos, brand names, slogans, visual identities. |
| **Protects What?** | **Expression** of an idea (the text/code). | The functional **idea/mechanism** itself. | Brand **distinction** in commerce. |
| **Registration Required?** | Automatic upon creation (registration is optional but legal proof). | Mandatory (granted only after patent office review). | Optional for ™; mandatory for ® legal protection. |
| **Duration in India** | Lifetime of creator + **60 years**. | **20 years** (non-renewable). | **10 years** (renewable indefinitely). |
| **Standard Example** | The Python source code of an app; a novel; a song. | A new battery cathode architecture; a drone motor design. | The Apple bite logo; the Nike Swoosh; the name "Tata". |

### Violations of Intellectual Property: Plagiarism vs. Infringement

*   **1. Plagiarism:**
    *   *Definition:* The act of copying another person's ideas, words, code, or creative work and presenting them as one's own original creation without acknowledging or citing the original author.
    *   *Nature:* It is an **academic and ethical violation** (a form of intellectual fraud/theft). It damages academic reputation and can lead to expulsion or dismissal.
    *   *Key Nuance:* You can commit plagiarism even if the original work is in the public domain (no copyright exists) if you dishonestly claim you wrote it.
*   **2. Copyright Infringement:**
    *   *Definition:* The unauthorized reproduction, distribution, public display, or commercial sale of a copyrighted work without obtaining legal permission or licence from the copyright holder.
    *   *Nature:* It is a **legal offense / civil and criminal violation** of statutory law (punishable under the Copyright Act with fines and imprisonment).
    *   *Examples:* Downloading pirated movies/games via torrents, distributing cracked copies of commercial software (Windows, Photoshop), copying proprietary code into a commercial product without a licence.
*   **3. Trademark Infringement:**
    *   *Definition:* The unauthorized use of a registered trademark (or a deceptively similar mark) on competing goods or services, misleading consumers regarding the origin or authenticity of the goods.

- **Comparison: Plagiarism vs. Copyright Infringement**

| Dimension | Plagiarism | Copyright Infringement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Type of Violation** | **Ethical / Academic** fraud. | **Legal / Statutory** crime. |
| **Core Offense** | Claiming authorship of someone else's work ("I wrote this"). | Using someone's work without permission/licence. |
| **Does Attribution Save You?** | **Yes:** Proper citation completely clears plagiarism. | **No:** Giving credit to the author while distributing pirated copies is still 100% copyright infringement. |
| **Governing Authority** | Academic boards, universities, institutions, professional bodies. | Courts of law, Police, Intellectual Property appellate boards. |
| **Penalty** | Zero grades, paper retraction, loss of job, expulsion. | Statutory financial damages, confiscation, imprisonment. |

- **Real Indian Examples:**
  1. *Basmati Rice & Turmeric Patent Disputes:* In the 1990s, foreign entities attempted to patent traditional Indian medicinal uses of turmeric and basmati rice lines in the US. India's Council of Scientific and Industrial Research (CSIR) successfully challenged and revoked these patents using ancient Sanskrit texts and the Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL).
  2. *Software Piracy Prosecution:* Indian software trade associations (e.g., NASSCOM) assisting police in raiding commercial establishments running thousands of unlicensed, cracked copies of CAD or engineering software without paying enterprise licences.
  3. *Academic Code Copying:* A college student submitting an open-source GitHub project for their final CBSE practical project, claiming they engineered the entire architecture from scratch without citing the repo (Plagiarism).

## 04. Open Source Software and Licences: Creative Commons, GPL, and Apache

- **Mental Model:**
  When a developer writes software, they automatically hold the copyright. By default, it is **Proprietary ("All Rights Reserved")**—nobody may view, modify, or share the source code.
  If the author wants the world to benefit, they release it under a **Free and Open Source Software (FOSS)** licence ("Some Rights Reserved"). The licence is the legal contract stating what the public is allowed and not allowed to do with that code.

- **Key Facts & CBSE Exam Definitions:**
  - **Proprietary Software (Closed Source):** Software whose source code is kept secret and proprietary by the vendor. Users purchase a licence to run the binary, but cannot inspect, modify, or redistribute the code (e.g., Microsoft Windows, macOS, Adobe Photoshop).
  - **FOSS (Free and Open Source Software):** Software that gives users the freedom to run, study, modify, and redistribute the source code.
    - *Free Software (FSF / Richard Stallman):* Focuses on user liberty/freedoms (Freedom 0 to run, Freedom 1 to study/modify, Freedom 2 to redistribute, Freedom 3 to distribute modified versions).
    - *Open Source (OSI):* Focuses on the practical, collaborative software engineering methodology.
  - **Freeware vs. Shareware vs. FOSS:**
    - *Freeware:* Available free of charge, but source code is NOT provided (e.g., Adobe Acrobat Reader, Skype).
    - *Shareware:* Distributed free on a trial basis for evaluation; expires or requires payment after a trial period (e.g., WinRAR).
    - *FOSS:* Source code is freely provided and permits modification and redistribution.

### Major Open Source Licences: Copyleft vs. Permissive

```
                         Open Source Licences
                                   |
                +------------------+------------------+
                |                                     |
       Copyleft / Reciprocal                  Permissive / Academic
    (GPL - General Public License)             (Apache 2.0, MIT, BSD)
    "Share-alike: derived code                 "Do whatever you want,
      MUST remain open source"                  even in closed commercial apps"
```

1. **GNU GPL (General Public License):**
   - Maintained by the Free Software Foundation (FSF).
   - **Strong Copyleft Licence:** Mandates that if you modify GPL-licensed code or link with it to build a derivative work, **your entire resulting software MUST also be released under the GPL licence with source code made public**.
   - Known as the **"Share-Alike" / Viral clause**: it prevents proprietary corporations from taking open code, adding features, closing the source, and selling it as a black-box proprietary product.
   - *Example:* The **Linux Kernel**, Git, Blender.

2. **Apache License (e.g., Apache 2.0):**
   - Maintained by the Apache Software Foundation.
   - **Permissive Open Source Licence:** Grants users sweeping permissions to use, modify, distribute, and sell the code.
   - *Crucial Difference from GPL:* **Does NOT require derived works to remain open source.** A corporation can take Apache-licensed code, modify it, keep the modifications proprietary, and sell it commercially.
   - *Requirements:* You must preserve the original copyright notice, include a copy of the Apache licence, state significant changes made, and adhere to an explicit grant of patent rights.
   - *Examples:* Android OS (AOSP), Apache HTTP Server, TensorFlow.

- **Comparison: Copyleft (GPL) vs. Permissive (Apache 2.0)**

| Dimension | GNU GPL (Copyleft) | Apache 2.0 (Permissive) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Philosophical Goal** | Guarantee permanent software freedom for all downstream users. | Maximum practical adoption and commercial reuse. |
| **Can Derivative Work be Closed-Source?** | **Strictly NO.** Any derived work must be open source under GPL. | **YES.** Companies can include it in closed-source proprietary products. |
| **Patent Protection Clause** | General patent clause (in GPLv3). | Explicit, dedicated patent grant protects contributors and users. |
| **Industry Adoption** | Loved by community activists; avoided by proprietary commercial enterprises. | Heavily adopted by Big Tech (Google, Apache, IBM) for broad corporate ecosystems. |
| **Standard Example** | Linux Kernel, GNU utilities, GIMP. | Android Open Source Project, Kubernetes, Apache Kafka. |

### Creative Commons (CC) Licences

Creative Commons is designed not for executable software code, but for **creative, cultural, educational, and artistic works** (photographs, videos, music, research papers, NCERT books, Wikipedia).

CC uses a modular combination of **four fundamental conditions / symbols**:
1. **BY (Attribution):** You must give appropriate credit to the creator, provide a link to the licence, and indicate if changes were made. (Present in almost all CC licences).
2. **NC (Non-Commercial):** The work may only be used, shared, or remixed for non-commercial purposes. You cannot sell it for profit.
3. **ND (No Derivatives):** You may copy and distribute the work, but you **cannot alter, remix, crop, translate, or transform** it.
4. **SA (Share-Alike):** If you remix, transform, or build upon the material, you must distribute your contributions under the **exact same licence** as the original (the CC equivalent of copyleft).

*Standard CC Licence Combinations:*
*   **CC0 (Public Domain Dedication):** No rights reserved; anyone can do anything without attribution.
*   **CC BY:** Most open; use for any purpose, even commercial, as long as credit is given.
*   **CC BY-SA:** Attribution + Share-Alike (used by **Wikipedia**).
*   **CC BY-NC-ND:** Most restrictive; must give credit, cannot sell, cannot alter.

- **Common Exam Traps:**
  - *Trap 1:* Thinking "Free Software" means zero price ($0 cost). As Richard Stallman stated: *"Think of free as in free speech, not as in free beer."* Free software refers to liberty/freedom to inspect and modify, not price.
  - *Trap 2:* Believing attribution makes copyright infringement legal. If you download a copyrighted film and give credit to the director, it is still illegal copyright infringement.
  - *Trap 3:* Confusing Apache with GPL. Apache allows closing source in derived products; GPL strictly forbids closing source.
